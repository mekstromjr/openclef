title: Voice & tone
description: How to write for OpenClef — voice attributes, tone by context, and do/don't pairs.
---

## Voice (constant)

- **Plainspoken** — short words over long ones; "open" not "facilitate."
- **Quietly confident** — we don't have to oversell.
- **Musically literate** — we say "movement," "opus," "voicing" without
  translating.
- **Unsentimental** — warmth comes from being useful, not from emoji.

## Tone (varies by context)

| Context | Tone | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Onboarding | Welcoming, direct | "Add your first piece. We'll handle the rest." |
| Empty state | Encouraging | "Your library is quiet. Upload a PDF to get started." |
| Error | Calm, accountable | "We couldn't read that file. It looks like a scanned image — try a PDF with embedded text." |
| Marketing | Evocative | "New tools for a timeless medium." |
| Release notes | Matter-of-fact | "Faster search. Better MusicXML import. Two bugs fewer." |

## Do / Don't

| Don't | Do |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Oops! Something went wrong 😬" | "We couldn't open that file. Here's why: …" |
| "Unleash your music library!" | "Find any piece in your library in seconds." |
| "Sync your scores to the cloud!" | "Your scores stay on your server." |
| "Powered by AI" | "Composer and title detected automatically." |
