# Setlists

A setlist is a running order: the pieces for one rehearsal, service or recital, in the order you will play them.

## Who can see and change a setlist

A setlist is yours: you see it, and so does anyone you [share your library](/docs/guides/sharing) with. Only the person who created a setlist, or an admin, can rename it or delete it.

## Sharing a setlist

Open the setlist and use **Sharing** at the bottom to share it with a person or with a group you're in:

- **Can view**: they see the setlist and play it.
- **Can add**: they can also add their own pieces and reorder it. They can remove only the pieces they added.

A shared setlist works like a shared folder: **everyone who sees it gets the pieces on it that whoever added them owns**, and nothing else. A piece someone shared with you doesn't pass on to your setlist's audience. So, on a shared setlist:

- everyone, the owner included, can add only pieces they own; **Add pieces** shows only your own music;
- if the setlist already holds pieces you don't own, sharing it first lists them and asks whether to share anyway. Its audience sees those entries as "a piece that isn't shared with you".

If someone who could add pieces loses the setlist (you stop sharing with them, or they leave the group you shared it through), the pieces they added stop reaching anyone else. You see those entries as "*Title* is no longer shared with you by *name*" and can remove them.

Only the owner shares a setlist; there is no admin override. Being someone's partner lets you see their setlists, not add to them.

## Making a setlist

1. Open **Setlists** in the top bar, type a name, and choose **Create**.
2. Choose **Add pieces**, search the library, and tick the pieces you want. They are added in the order you tick them.
3. Reorder with the up and down arrows, or drag a row on a desktop. Choose **Save order** when you are done; **Discard** puts it back.

You can also add a piece from its own page with **Add to setlist**. If you were looking at a particular part (the cello part, say), that part is the one the setlist opens.

The same piece can appear more than once, for example a hymn at the start and again at the end.

## Choosing which part opens

When a piece has several parts, each row has an **Opens** menu. The setlist opens that part when you play. If the chosen part is later deleted, the setlist falls back to the piece's first part.

## Playing a setlist

Choose **Play** on a setlist, or the play arrow on a row to start from that piece. Entries you can't open are skipped.

- Turn pages as usual: tap the right side, swipe, or press Space, an arrow key or Page Down.
- On the last page a banner shows what comes next. Turning once more opens the next piece at page 1.
- Turning back from page 1 opens the previous piece at its last page.
- The top menu (tap the top strip) has a **Piece** list to jump anywhere in the setlist.
- Fullscreen stays on from piece to piece, and the next piece is downloaded while you play the current one.

The address bar records where you are, so reloading the page, or opening the same link on another device, resumes at the same piece.

## Deleting

Deleting a setlist never deletes the pieces in it.

A piece can be deleted from the library while it is on setlists. Its entry stays in each one as "*Title* was deleted by *name*", so the gap in the running order is visible. Before you delete, the piece's page asks for confirmation and says how many setlists it is on.

## Pieces you can't open

An entry can stay on a setlist after you lose the piece:

- **Deleted:** "*Title* was deleted by *name*".
- **No longer shared:** "*Title* is no longer shared with you by *name*", when the piece's owner stops sharing it. If they share it again, the entry works again.

You see the title only if you could see the piece before. Anyone else sees "A piece that isn't shared with you" in its place. **Play** skips all of these entries. The setlist's owner can move them or remove them with ✕, and saving the order keeps them as they are.

## When two people edit at once

If someone else saves a setlist while you have it open, your next save is refused with a message to reload, rather than quietly overwriting their changes.
