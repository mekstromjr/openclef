# Setlists

A setlist is a running order: the pieces for one rehearsal, service or recital, in the order you will play them.

## Who can see and change a setlist

Everyone signed in sees every setlist and can play it. Only the person who created a setlist, or an admin, can rename it, reorder it, add or remove pieces, or delete it.

## Making a setlist

1. Open **Setlists** in the top bar, type a name, and choose **Create**.
2. Choose **Add pieces**, search the library, and tick the pieces you want. They are added in the order you tick them.
3. Reorder with the up and down arrows, or drag a row on a desktop. Choose **Save order** when you are done; **Discard** puts it back.

You can also add a piece from its own page with **Add to setlist**. If you were looking at a particular part (the cello part, say), that part is the one the setlist opens.

The same piece can appear more than once, for example a hymn at the start and again at the end.

## Choosing which part opens

When a piece has several parts, each row has an **Opens** menu. The setlist opens that part when you play. If the chosen part is later deleted, the setlist falls back to the piece's first part.

## Playing a setlist

Choose **Play** on a setlist, or the play arrow on a row to start from that piece.

- Turn pages as usual: tap the right side, swipe, or press Space, an arrow key or Page Down.
- On the last page a banner shows what comes next. Turning once more opens the next piece at page 1.
- Turning back from page 1 opens the previous piece at its last page.
- The top menu (tap the top strip) has a **Piece** list to jump anywhere in the setlist.
- Fullscreen stays on from piece to piece, and the next piece is downloaded while you play the current one.

The address bar records where you are, so reloading the page, or opening the same link on another device, resumes at the same piece.

## Deleting

Deleting a setlist never deletes the pieces in it. A piece that is on a setlist can't be deleted from the library until it has been removed from every setlist; the piece's page lists which setlists it is on.

## When two people edit at once

If someone else saves a setlist while you have it open, your next save is refused with a message to reload, rather than quietly overwriting their changes.
