# Sharing

Your library is private until you share it. There are two ways:

- **Share your library** with a person, like a photo app's partner sharing: they see all of your music.
- **Share chosen pieces with a group**, such as a choir or a band: its members see those pieces and nothing else of yours.

## Sharing your library

1. Open **Settings** from your account menu.
2. Under **Sharing**, choose a person in **I share my library with**, then **Share**.

They can now find, play and edit everything you own, and put it on their setlists. They can't delete your music or pass it on to anyone else.

Sharing is one way. Sharing with someone doesn't let you see their music; they have to share with you too. And it doesn't chain: if you share with Sam and Sam shares with Alex, Alex sees Sam's music, not yours.

To stop, choose **Stop sharing** next to their name. Your pieces leave their library and stop opening from their setlists.

## Groups

Anyone can create a group:

1. Open **Settings** from your account menu.
2. Under **Groups**, type a name and choose **Create group**.
3. Pick people in **Add a person…** and choose **Add**.

You run the groups you create: you can rename them, add and remove people, and delete them. If someone else runs a group you're in, you can **Leave** it.

To share a piece with a group, open the piece and tick the group next to **Share with**. Only the piece's owner sees these boxes, and only for groups they're in. Untick to stop sharing it.

Members can find, play and edit what's shared with the group, and put it on their setlists. Each group's name in Settings opens its page: everything shared with it, with the library's search and filters.

When someone leaves or is removed, the group's music disappears for them, and the pieces and setlists they shared with it leave the group. Deleting a group stops everything it shared, but the music stays with its owners.

## Sharing a setlist

A setlist can be shared with people or groups, to view or to add their own pieces. Whoever sees it gets only the pieces on it that the person who added them owns, so a setlist never hands on music someone shared with you. See [Setlists](/docs/guides/setlists#sharing-a-setlist).

## Music shared with you

Pieces someone else shares with you, directly or through a group, appear in your library with a small people icon. Hover over it, or open the piece, to see who shared it.

Under **Shared with me** in Settings:

- **Show in my library** decides whether that person's music appears in your main library. Turning it off doesn't remove anything: it stays on their page and in your setlists. Pieces they share with a group you're in still show, because the group shares them with you. It also leaves their music out of a setlist's **Add pieces** search; to add one of their pieces, open it from their page and choose **Add to setlist**.
- The person's name opens their page: everything they share with you, with the same search and filters as your library. Names in a group's member list open the same page.

## Only my music

**Only my music** at the top of the library hides everything shared with you, so you see just the pieces you added.

## Deleting a shared piece

Only the person who owns a piece, or an admin, can delete it, even while it is on setlists. The confirmation says how many setlists hold it, including ones you can't see, such as a partner's setlist.

Each of those setlists keeps an entry that says "*Title* was deleted by *name*". The title is shown only to people who saw the piece: the setlist's owner, whoever added the piece, and the setlist's audience if the piece had reached them. Everyone else sees only that a piece isn't shared with them.

When you stop sharing a piece instead, a setlist that holds it says "*Title* is no longer shared with you by *name*" to the people who put it there.
